# Competitor copy research — July 2026

Who else does what Taborlin does (small studio building + operating multiple AI/agent
products), how they talk about it, and what we should steal.

## The comparables

### Every (every.to) — media company + AI product studio
Closest structural comparable: small team shipping a bundle of focused AI products
(Cora, Spiral, Sparkle, Monologue) under one brand.

- Homepage: "The only subscription you need to stay at the edge of AI. Ideas, apps,
  and training from practitioners who build with AI daily."
- Product one-liners give each product a human job, not a feature list:
  - Cora: "your AI chief of staff who maintains your inbox"
  - Spiral: "The writing partner for you and your agent"
  - Monologue: "Effortless voice dictation"
- Studio framing: "a bundle of AI apps — creative tools to expand your mind and help
  you do your best work."

**Lesson:** one clear buyer-benefit umbrella line, then one plain-English job per
product. Never lead with the stack.

### Answer.AI — practical AI R&D lab (Jeremy Howard / Eric Ries)
- Tagline: "Practical AI R&D" — "a new kind of AI R&D lab which creates practical
  end-user products based on foundational research breakthroughs."
- Memorable frame: "a new old kind of R&D lab" (Edison's invention lab analogy).

**Lesson:** a crisp two-word identity ("Practical AI R&D") beats a paragraph. Claim a
category shape people already understand, with one twist.

### 37signals — the original small multi-product studio
- Site is a catalog of opinions/principles, not a feature tour. Proudly small,
  no-VC, plain-spoken. Products described by feel and outcome: HEY is "A delightfully
  fresh take on email + calendar."

**Lesson:** for a studio site, opinionated identity IS the product. Small team is a
brag, not a caveat. Short declarative sentences.

### Sierra (sierra.ai) — enterprise AI agents
- Homepage title: "Better customer experiences" — the outcome, not the tech. Agents
  are the how: "personal, proactive, and act independently… optimizing for their
  happiness and your business goals."

**Lesson:** outcome first; "agent" is subordinate. Nobody buys "agent-run" — they buy
what the agent gets done.

### Lindy — AI agents for business work
- "AI agents that do the work for you." / "Lindy doesn't assist. It acts." /
  "No code. No bloat. No headcount required."

**Lesson:** verbs and short negations. "Do the work" is the strongest agent phrase in
the market right now.

### Cognition (Devin)
- "The first AI software engineer." Single bold category claim.

**Lesson:** if you can claim a category in ≤6 words, do it.

## What's wrong with our current copy

1. **Hero is insider-speak.** "Agent-run software products, operated in the real
   world" describes our architecture, not a benefit. Every winning comparable leads
   with the outcome (Sierra) or a plain identity (Answer.AI).
2. **Subhead is an infrastructure list.** "payments, approvals, metrics, and
   production ops baked in" — these are proof points, not a pitch.
3. **The stats section has no stats.** Cards read "Products / Tools / Ops" where a
   number should be. Real numbers (3 live products, 2 open-source tools) are more
   credible than category words.
4. **Product blurbs mix jobs with plumbing.** e.g. CheapTokens leads with the job but
   ends in "x402, card settlement, key issuance, account flows, and reconciliation."
5. **Capabilities reads as a personal résumé** ("Enterprise Holdings deal
   structuring") on a company-voiced page.

## Copy principles adopted

- Lead with the work the products do; "agents" explains how, "humans approve" builds
  trust (our genuine differentiator — approval-first is all over our products).
- One-sentence job per product, Every-style; plumbing details demoted or cut.
- Real numbers in the stats band.
- Small studio framed as a strength, 37signals-style.

## Hero options considered

- A (chosen): "AI products that do real work. / Agents run them. Humans sign off."
- B: "A small studio. Software that runs itself." — cute but undercuts credibility.
- C: "Practical agent software." — Answer.AI homage, too abstract alone.

## Addendum: consulting practice

Taborlin also consults — current clients are WeatherXM (decentralized weather
station network, DePIN) and Sorcerer (YC S24, long-duration weather balloons for
atmospheric data). This explains the weather/geo DNA on the site (wind API,
WindMode, micromarkets, Spatix) and the energy/infrastructure language in
Capabilities. Per Alex, client names stay off the site. Final identity frame:
Taborlin is three things — a product studio, labs (open-source tools and
experiments), and a consulting firm. The stats band now states that tripod
(Studio / Labs / Consulting) instead of numbers, and the Capabilities section
is "Consulting & Capabilities" with no client names.
